Privacy policy

Last updated: 22 November 2026

AI Photo Book is made by Danger Island ApS, Th. Staunings Vej 48, 9210 Aalborg SØ, Denmark, CVR __CVR__ ("we"). Questions about privacy: __EMAIL__.

The short version

  • Your photos stay on your iPhone. The app finds trips, picks photos and lays out your book on the phone. We never receive your photos, thumbnails or anything the app works out about them.
  • No account, no tracking, no analytics, no ads. The app works without signing up.
  • The few things that do leave the phone are listed below, each with what is sent, to whom and why.

What stays on your iPhone

Everything the app works with is stored only on your iPhone, in the app's own storage:

  • your photo library as the app reads it (dates, places and sizes of photos), and its analysis of each photo (sharpness, faces, near-duplicates), all worked out on the phone with Apple's on-device frameworks;
  • copies of photos from shared albums and of photos you share to the app from other apps;
  • your books, their texts, chosen printers and settings;
  • which books you have bought print files for.

Deleting the app deletes all of this. We have no copy.

What leaves your iPhone, and why

1. Place names (Apple)

To name trips ("Lisbon, July 2026") the app asks Apple's map service for a place name: one coordinate per place, rounded to about 1 km, without the photo or anything else. Answers are kept on the phone so each place is asked once. You can turn this off in Settings → "Look up place names"; trips are then named by their dates. Recipient: Apple (Apple's privacy policy applies).

2. Buying print files (Apple)

Print files are bought through Apple's App Store. The app sends Apple the product and a random code for each purchase attempt, so it can tell which book a purchase was for. It never sends a photo or anything about your book. Payment, your Apple ID and receipts are handled by Apple; we don't receive your payment details. Recipient: Apple.

3. Your own photos from iCloud

If a photo is stored only in iCloud Photos, showing it or making a print file asks your iPhone to download the original from your own iCloud to your phone. Nothing is sent to us; it is a download of your own photo. Recipient: Apple (your iCloud).

4. Printers' websites

The app can open a printer's ordering page in Safari. The app itself sends nothing; you upload your files yourself, from the share sheet, and the printer's own privacy policy applies to what you send them.

5. What you share

Videos, pages and cards you make in the app go only where you send them, from the iOS share sheet. Their text includes a link to AI Photo Book on the App Store; the app sends nothing itself.

6. Notifications

If you allow them, notifications ("Your Lisbon trip is ready") are created on your iPhone. There is no notification server, and nothing is sent.

What we don't do

No advertising, no selling or sharing of data with advertisers or data brokers, no tracking across apps or websites, no analytics, no crash reports that upload data, and no face recognition beyond what your iPhone does on the phone to compare faces within your own photos.

Apple may send us crash reports and usage numbers from people who chose to share them with app developers in their iPhone's settings. These come through Apple, contain no photos, and aren't linked to you.

Legal basis (GDPR)

Place names and purchases: what is needed to provide the app you chose to use (Art. 6(1)(b)).

Your rights

You can ask us for access to, correction of, deletion of or a copy of data we hold about you, and object to or limit its use. Because we hold almost nothing and nothing is linked to your name, there is usually nothing to look up. Write to __EMAIL__. You can also complain to the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet, datatilsynet.dk).

Children

The app isn't aimed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect anything from them. In-app purchases follow Apple's Family Sharing and Ask to Buy rules.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published here with a new date. For a change that sends anything new from your phone, the app asks you first.
